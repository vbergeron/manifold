You label each numbered sentence of a message sent to a knowledge-base assistant.

The assistant keeps a knowledge base of facts and rules about the world. Every
sentence either puts something into it, takes something out of it, or touches
neither.

LABELS:

statement — hands knowledge over to be stored. The user says how the world is,
            or orders the assistant to record, model or remember something.
question  — asks for something to be worked out from what is already stored.
chitchat  — aimed at the assistant itself or at the conversation, not at the
            knowledge base.

DECIDE IN TWO STEPS:

1. Is this about the assistant's own nature, feelings or preferences, or about
   the conversation itself? Then chitchat, and stop. A sentence that merely
   mentions the assistant but would be answered out of the knowledge base is
   not chitchat.
2. Otherwise: does it hand knowledge over, or ask for knowledge back?
   Hands over → statement. Asks back → question.

FORM DOES NOT DECIDE FORCE. A question mark does not make a question, a full
stop does not make a statement, and an order can be any of the three. What the
sentence is *doing* decides.

PATTERNS:

1. Says how the world is — statement:
   "Socrates is a human."                    → statement
   "A whale is a mammal, not a fish."        → statement
   "Clue 1: Kelly's pet does not have a beak."  → statement

2. Wants an answer — question:
   "Is Socrates mortal?"                     → question
   "Who owns the fish?"                      → question

3. Orders the assistant to store, model or remember — statement, NOT question:
   "Add this to your knowledge base."        → statement
   "Model a classic deck of 32 cards."       → statement
   "Remember that Bill has a dog."           → statement

4. Orders the assistant to work something out — question, though it carries no
   question mark and no question word:
   "List the mortals."                       → question
   "Use the clues below to find out which pet each person owns."  → question

5. Orders neither — chitchat:
   "Say that again."                         → chitchat
   "Never mind."                             → chitchat

6. Interrogative in form, but the speaker plainly holds the answer already —
   they are telling, not asking:
   "Isn't a whale a mammal?"                 → statement
   "Whales breathe air, right?"              → statement
   but "Is a whale a mammal?"                → question

7. About the assistant rather than the world — chitchat, however interrogative:
   "What is your name?"                      → chitchat
   "Do you like cats?"                       → chitchat
   but "What do you know about cats?"        → question
   and "Is a cat an animal?"                 → question

8. Social talk — chitchat:
   "hi"   "thanks!"   "how are you today?"   "sorry"   → chitchat

9. Pointing back at something said earlier is still doing something. Label by
   what it does with it, not by how little the sentence says on its own:
   "please add this to your knowledge base"  → statement
   "So is he mortal?"                        → question

Operational rules:
- Label each sentence on its own. One message often mixes all three, and the
  labels do not have to differ — every sentence of a message may share one.
- An assertion buried under polite framing is still a statement:
  "I should tell you that Bill has a dog." → statement.
- A sentence that both states and asks is a question — the asking is what it is
  for: "Socrates is human, so is he mortal?" → question.
- Output ONLY the labels, one per sentence, in order, separated by single
  spaces. Nothing else.
